Cascade Decay Frames

Decay Description

Kind of Decay

Momenta

Energy and momentum components of the mother 0 and of the final states 1, 2, … in the laboratory frame, in any common unit. The momentum of the mother is the sum of the final states; the mass of every particle follows from its energy and momentum.

Topology

The topology is an ordered binary tree over the numbers 1, 2, … of the final states. Every pair stands in brackets, its two parts are separated by a space, and a part is a number or another pair:

  • (1 2) for two final states,
  • ((1 2) 3): the pair of 1 and 2 first, then 3,
  • ((1 3) (2 4)): two pairs.

The first part of a pair is the reference particle, the second the spectator. Every number occurs exactly once.

Reference Topology (optional)

A second topology is the reference topology and gets the label I; the chain of the topology above then gets the name II, which can be changed in the field next to “Topology”. Without a second topology the topology above is the reference topology, named I (the field is grey).

Notation of the frame labels

A quantity annotated by [v|K] is expressed in the frame v|K, for example p1[(12)|I], the spatial momentum of particle 1 in the frame [(12)|I].

v
The vertex whose parent is at rest in this frame. The frame is named by that vertex: (12) is the rest frame of the intermediate state (12)i, the system of the particles 1p and 2p. R0I is the rest frame of the mother 0p with the common root axes oriented by topology I.
K
The topology, labeled by a Roman numeral, whose ordered transports lead to the frame. [R0I|I] and [R0I|II] denote identical axes, whereas [(12)|I] and [(12)|II] may differ.
lab
[lab] is the specified starting frame of the event, not an absolute rest frame.
Axes
The root z axis is the laboratory flight direction of the mother. The root x axis points along the transverse momentum of the first daughter of the reference topology, and y completes a right-handed system. This is the Greenwich convention, and it is the reference topology that fixes it: every other topology uses the same root axes and does not choose an x axis of its own, so the first daughter of a different decay topology generally has a nonzero azimuth in them. The page “Greenwich convention” explains the rotation Rz(−χ) that sets this choice, in analogy to the zero meridian of the Earth. Every later frame inherits its z axis from the momentum direction in its parent frame; the azimuth is not reset.
Signs
Rotations turn vectors and states, with positive angles. A coordinate system is parametrised by the inverse rotation: to express the momenta in axes that are turned by φ about z, their coordinates are transformed with Rz(−φ), and in the same way with Ry(−ϑ); for a frame that moves with the rapidity η they are boosted with Bz(−η). This is why the steps of the dynamic view carry negative angles and rapidities. The rotation R = Rz(α) Ry(β) Rz(γ) of the page “Next step” is a rotation of vectors and therefore has the positive angles. The section “Euler rotations” on the page “Greenwich convention” explains the convention.
Angles
For a decay A → B + C, the first daughter B is the reference and C the spectator (the upper and the lower line of the vertex in the sketch of the topology). A particle is written 1p, an intermediate state (12)i, a vertex (12)v, named after everything that is produced there. The angles φB and ϑB are the azimuth and polar angle of B, and η denotes a rapidity, all measured in the parent frame. A subscript such as 12 denotes the subsystem sum p12 = p1 + p2 in one common frame.
